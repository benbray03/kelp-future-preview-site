Kelp Future Preview

An interactive mapping tool for exploring future projections of kelp density

About

Kelp Future Preview was created by the Caselle Lab at the University of California, Santa Barbara through a California Sea Grant funded project.

Kelp Future Preview (henceforth "the app") is an interactive mapping tool for visualizing future projections of giant kelp (Macrocystis pyrifera) and bull kelp (Nereocystis luetkeana) density along the coast of California through 2100. The future kelp maps are based on 20+ years of in-situ kelp forest monitoring through the Partnership for Interdisciplinary Studies of Coastal Oceans (PISCO) and Reef Check, combined with California-specific environmental drivers of kelp density derived from three global earth-ocean-atmosphere models, also called Earth System Models (ESMs). Maps are displayed at 300 m resolution and allow users to interactively select between kelp density and relevant environmental drivers at 5-year increments.

The intended users of the app are marine scientists, resource managers, marine spatial planners, and community members.

The app can be used to gain insight into the potential trajectories of giant and bull kelp in California. Future projections are grouped into 5-year increments to reduce focus on predictions for a specific year and location. These projections should not be taken to mean that a specific density will be observed at a particular future time point. Results shown are the ensemble mean of three ESMs.

Note: Specific values should not be extracted from the map and used in isolation to support state public comment or petitions.

How to Use the Map

  1. Choose one of two species using the species buttons.
  2. Choose a map layer to display. You may select modeled kelp density or one of the underlying environmental predictors.
  3. Use the year slider (or press play) to step through projected years.
  4. The left-side panel plots update to summarize the currently displayed layer.
  5. Toggle additional static layers on or off, including the network of marine protected areas (MPAs).
  6. The orange dashed line marks the 50% density latitude: half of the species' predicted kelp density lies south of it and half north. The grey dotted line shows where it was in the first projection year.
